## 1. Desired Need and current use mode

I want to explore prepared simulations of how a changing application and the work of evolving it unfold under different legitimate architectures, and understand why those architectures have different costs, schedules, available options, implementation consequences and follow-on business/product outcomes. The tool should make actual history, requirement knowledge, architecture/implementation state, planning, contingent futures and work quality inspectable without a hidden universal winner.

**User-confirmed current scope:** Simulation authors script the case: initial application needs, architecture alternatives, shared and branch-specific events, plausible causal effects, implementation and planning histories. An end user primarily **observes, navigates, explains and compares** the prepared alternatives; they are not required to author new architectures or dynamically simulate unprepared outcomes. Branching and conditional plans are still useful inside scripted material. Interactive architecture planning for a particular real application may come later; no IDE, architecture editor, Monte Carlo or real-code generation is presently entailed.

## 2. Truth boundaries and comparison

1. **Shared scenario backbone, causal divergence.** Compared architectures begin at **one shared development starting point** with the same independent situation, externally provided needs and constraints; **situational/exogenous events that do not depend on architectural choice remain the same** for compared architectures. Scripted branch outcomes may diverge where prior architecture, implementation readiness, duration, business opportunity or other explained state causally affects what happens next. An author must not give favorable or unfavorable architecture-independent events to just one compared architecture. Differences in later facts must trace to architectural implementation, work/readiness/timing or downstream causal effects, not arbitrary alternative external scripts. Fair comparison does **not** mean every later event or requirement is identical.
2. **One fictional calendar may be used.** Simulation dates/times and durations make parallel histories readable. Event order/causal dependency, elapsed time and a calendar timestamp are distinct dimensions; invented durations and estimates are scenario assumptions, not empirically proven universal architecture performance.
3. **Factual vs planned truth.** Actual Event History records what happened, when and to which compared architecture/branch. Requirement knowledge, factual architecture and fake implementation are reconstructed at the relevant factual cursor. A planned Evolution Step, anticipated event or deadline is **not** itself an Actual Event of execution/completion. Only a later actual outcome establishes actual completion, lateness, customer loss, etc.
4. **No hindsight.** The *simulation author* can know all scripted futures; the *fictional team at planning time* can use only information then known, expected, forecast, or expressed as uncertainty. An unexpected future event must not be secretly encoded as a known detailed plan; generic contingency/reserve can be planned. Historical plan revisions preserve forecasts and their grounds, including conditional Steps and Planned Change Axes. The author knowing scripted outcomes does not give the fictional team hindsight.
5. **Branches may accumulate different known requirements.** Shared initial/externally supplied requirements are comparable across architectures. Requirements revealed through genuinely different branch outcomes need not appear in both branch-local factual models. `CDC-10@r6` must be qualified, not silently erased. The existing non-normalized requirement occurrence rule remains.

**Illustrative prepared scenario (not a mandatory domain fixture):** Both branches hear about a potential enterprise contract on April 1. A has delivered an enterprise capability; B is still migrating. A signs and receives new onboarding requirements; B misses the opportunity and replans. All outcomes are predetermined in the demonstration, with inspectable explanations; the fictional team could not know which outcome would occur ahead of time.

## 3. One Plan: user-accepted semantic boundary

**User decision (accepted): there is NO separate Evolution Map object.** The single plan for system development, referred to here as **Plan / PlanRevision** (working name, not a frozen API/schema), contains planned Evolution Steps alongside other planned work, anticipated/possible events, conditions, timing, deadlines, branches, forecasts and preparedness. The historical `EvolutionMapRevision` and stand-alone Evolution Map from r7/R11 are **legacy candidate proposals requiring revision**, not a second source of plan truth. A diagram of Step evolution may later be projected from Plan for navigation, but it is NOT a separately stored plan and is not required by this decision.

**Evolution Step (explicit user meaning):** any planned action that **changes or extends application functionality or the implementation/code/realization**, including refactoring, bug fixes, restructuring or migration where applicable. Work without such a change (e.g., negotiation, risk assessment, test-only verification, coordination, external waiting) can exist within the Plan without being mislabeled as an Evolution Step. Planning may group/display work differently, but does not silently convert unrelated activities into changes of realization.

**Preserve r7 semantics unless deliberately revised:** each planned Evolution Step still exposes a **complete target ArchitectureSnapshot after the Step**, not merely a patch, plus distinct EvolutionImpact. Code/implementation changes need not change the architectural structure; an unchanged architecture snapshot does not imply no work, no implementation effect, or no costs. Plan revisions have an immutable factual BASE and immutable plan-defining versions/history of Step schedules, routes, dependencies, expected snapshots and impacts; effective candidate P-/CDC- rules are stated in the companion r9 amendment; exact storage mechanics remain subject to prototype evidence.

### Planned events, predicted requirements and conditional outcomes

- A Plan can contain an **anticipated concrete event** because the fictional team believes it may or will occur and can describe its **expected impact**. That impact can select one of the conditional Evolution Step routes, alter timing/deadlines, induce replanning or, for example, introduce a concrete requirement. Events may be expected, optional, or contingent; their presence in a Plan does not make them factual Actual Events.
- **Concrete predicted requirement:** an anticipated event's expected result may be a specific possible new requirement. Mark it **forecast/conditional**, not factually known as an actual demand merely because it is in a Plan. Conversely, a future-delivery requirement already explicitly communicated to the team is **known in its historical Requirement Model**, even if work remains planned; use the information-availability rule (FR-8/9/27), not the implementation date.
- **Planned Change Axes** capture **less-concrete predicted directions of change** and which product/requirement areas are likely to experience future demands, not fabricated detailed future requirements. They can inform preparedness, optional Steps and structure decisions without pretending to know a future specification.
- **High project dynamism must be retained as first-class scenario context.** When product change is frequent/volatile or relevant new evidence arrives, the team may reconsider which Change Axes matter, how strongly, their time horizons and preparedness. Axes may arise, weaken, intensify, split, be replaced or cease to be relevant across immutable PlanRevisions. Neither a current Axis nor later observed `ActualHotPath` retroactively overwrites the historical forecast; distinguish predicted change direction from actually observed repeated changes.
- Anticipated events and current evidence can **qualitatively change the relative likelihood of a route** (more/less likely, dominant, uncertain) without precise percentages or automatically resolving the branch. Changed belief is not occurrence. When a condition is factually satisfied, the associated planned route can become applicable but is not thereby executed; execution remains recorded in Actual Event History.
- **Conditional/optional Evolution Steps** can be expressed as ordinary Steps with `IF` applicability conditions. A single condition can prepare several Steps. A possible change not yet detailed enough to be a Step can remain a forecast/uncertainty note in the Plan; no separate mandatory `EvolutionOption` object or activation workflow is needed for the first validation. Reconsider only if a concrete independent option lifecycle cannot be preserved through Plan revisions, notes and conditional Steps. No generic rules language or probabilistic engine is required.

### Timing, deadlines, fitness and comparison

A Plan can place Steps, other planned activities, expected events and conditions in a fictional calendar or relative schedule; it can carry dependencies, estimates, deadlines and their provenance. **An enduring deadline identity may have a revised target date with immutable history.** Separately, a changed *forecast completion date* is not a rescheduling of an externally fixed deadline; an actual missed deadline is only established by factual history.

For every material contemplated conditional route, the simulator should preserve route-relative predecessor/target states, applicability and anticipated effects, and show **conditional future Evolution Impacts, Evolution Fitness, feasibility/consistency and preparedness trade-offs**. A route may be more probable but not certain, and the architecture need not be equally optimized for every scenario. No forced single fitness scalar or calibrated probabilities.

**Evolution Fitness — explicit user-grounded meaning:** at a selected *actual or planned state*, or for a *contemplated implementation/code change*, ask whether delivering a specific requirement or following a prepared route would require **awkward, forced, unexpected or costly migrations**, and how the current/proposed state or change **fits the evolution work and Change Axes the team had planned at this historical point**. Viewpoints can be the current system, a local proposed change, a Step result, one conditional Plan route, or actual development examined later. The evaluation must name the requirement/goal, PlanRevision/route and evidentiary basis, separate migration and other cost classes, and distinguish knowingly planned migration from harmful surprise. A divergence from a plan can be justified when the plan is obsolete; Fitness does not automatically reward following obsolete intent or punish rational migration. This is **not a global architecture-quality score**.

**Fair cross-architecture comparison:** one shared point of departure and **the same architecture-independent situational events** (including calendar anchor, relevant demand, opportunity or external deadline). After that, **only causally explained architecture-dependent consequences** may differ, including completion time, business outcomes, later dependent events and new known requirements. Keep the distinct time cursor, scenario event anchor and branch-local factual event cursor concepts inspectable; no arbitrary branch-favoring external event differences.

## 4. Desired Scenario (prepared scripted case, observer/comparator)

The ten navigation opportunities below form **one nonrecursive demonstration journey**, not ten mandatory screens or mandatory actions in every case. The Stage A prototype tests only the minimal subset identified by §6 of `V3A-candidate-r9-delta`; subsequent stages validate the remaining capabilities without removing them from the intended outcome.

1. **Enter:** choose a prepared case, see a shared development starting point, architecture alternatives, scenario calendar and shared architecture-independent events.
2. **Inspect factual state:** at a date/common event and selected architecture branch, see only the factual requirement knowledge, architecture, fake file tree and current historical PlanRevision, with non-normalized requirements and event provenance.
3. **Inspect the Plan:** see its immutable BASE, concrete Steps changing function or code/implementation, other planned work, deadlines, possible events and conditions. Distinguish what the fictional team knew, forecast, considered uncertain or could not know.
4. **Explore planned event impacts:** an anticipated concrete event can forecast a specific requirement, alter branch choice, trigger an intended response or lead to changed expectations. A generic uncertainty is represented instead by a Planned Change Axis. Neither is an Actual Event until factually observed.
5. **Observe change dynamics:** under high product dynamism, historical plan revisions may change the set, salience and direction of Planned Change Axes; users can compare earlier predictions with later ActualHotPaths without hindsight.
6. **Explore contingent trajectories:** choose any prepared main or fallback conditional route, with its planned Steps, possible events, timing, time constraints and qualitative expectation of likelihood. Inspect a full ArchitectureSnapshot and separate EvolutionImpact after every Step on that route.
7. **Inspect impacts, Fitness and work:** choose a selected factual or planned architecture state or proposed change, inspect whether fulfilling a concrete requirement would force costly/awkward migration and whether it conflicts with later planned Steps/axes on each relevant conditional route; select architecture-native responsibility/file and see earlier and *conditional* later impacts. From one change/goal, examine its associated planned/actual analysis, implementation, testing, migration, coordination, delay and separate cost classes (accepted R10 PR-2), without assuming each is necessarily an Evolution Step.
8. **Move factual time:** see shared external events, distinct architecture-specific responses, possible deadline misses, branch-dependent requirements and planning/replanning Actual Events. Historical forecasts, PlanRevisions and immutable Step versions do not silently change. A condition becoming true does not auto-complete planned work.
9. **Compare architectures:** compare their reactions to the same independent external circumstances at common calendar/scenario anchors, and explain readiness, costs, changed probabilities/contingencies, route preparedness, resulting event divergence and requirement differences that have an actual causal explanation.
10. **Conclude:** explain conditional preferences, uncertainty, trade-offs and evidence without hidden ranking, ungrounded numeric probabilities or hindsight.

**Observable outcome:** a reader can trace common application circumstances → different architecture/implementation → Plans with Step work, possible events and evolving Change Axes → conditional routes / schedules / forecast requirements → factual events and architectural work → explained downstream differences, while keeping forecast and actuality separate.

## 6. Top-level Scenario Realization Units (candidate, not storage classes)

| ID | Contribution | Observable success |
|---|---|---|
| `SRU-1` Factual timeline/replay | Shared scenario anchors, one independent event backbone and branch-local factual histories with fictional calendar | Exact branch/time selection reproduces known requirements, actual state and available plan without hindsight. |
| `SRU-2` Requirement understanding | Non-normalized factual knowledge, including causal branch-local requirements and clear concrete forecasts | Actual requirement vs predicted possible requirement is never misrepresented. |
| `SRU-3` Architecture interpretation | Architecture-native, factual/planned complete snapshots, no universal BRU ontology | Multiple valid organization and reuse approaches are inspectable. |
| `SRU-4` Single conditional Plan | PlanRevisions with scheduled Evolution Steps, other work, anticipated events, branching, optional Steps, Change Axes, deadlines, immutable BASE/versions | One Plan is the planning truth; each Step has its full target snapshot, impacts and route provenance. |
| `SRU-5` Implementation projection | Fictional factual project/file tree and expected changes from Steps | Code/implementation changes can be visible even when architecture structure is unchanged. |
| `SRU-6` Comparative work/evolution evidence | Per-change work journey, separated cost classes, deadlines, likelihood shifts, uncertainty, cross-route Fitness and causal downstream effects | For a selected state/proposed change and specified route, explain forced/awkward/costly migrations **and alignment or conflict with the historical Plan**, plus work/outcomes, with inspectable evidence rather than a hidden universal score. |
| `SRU-7` Discoverable workbench | Clear factual context, Requirements, Plan, Architecture, Implementation and contextual inspectors | No separate Evolution Map data model or forced GUI tab; common vs branch-specific and predicted vs actual remain clear. |

**Scope/cost:** the full validation campaign (staged in a single PT plan) extends one scripted fixture with shared independent event(s), two architecture reactions, one conditional anticipated event, a specific predicted requirement, at least one change axis whose salience shifts when project dynamism/evidence changes, a deadline revision vs forecast revision distinction, and conditionally impacted Steps. Do not invent numerical probabilities or mandate an automatic simulator/scheduler.

## 7. Status, provenance and unresolved design details

**User-confirmed:** prepared explorer now; later application-specific planner deferred; single shared development starting point and identical architecture-independent events; only causally explained outcome divergence; **one Plan, no separate Evolution Map**; Evolution Steps are changes to function or implementation/code; Plan can also contain other work, possible/expected events and their impacts, predicted concrete requirements, conditional Step routes, optional Steps, qualitative shifts in likely routes, planned time and deadlines, and **mutable-by-revision Planned Change Axes under high project dynamism**; historical knowledge/no-hindsight; conditional cross-route fitness/impacts; **Evolution Fitness as costly/awkward required migration + fit of a selected state/proposed change to historical planned evolution**, not a global score; R10 PR-2 work exploration.

**Candidate technical terminology:** `PlanRevision` is a working name replacing the previous `EvolutionMapRevision` concept. The decision is single-plan semantics, **not** endorsement of an exact class, event grammar, probability engine or Plan schema.
